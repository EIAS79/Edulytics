**Warm-up (10 min):** Notice and Wonder: *Two Sets of Angles*. Show legitimately accessible original drawings or newly independently authored equivalents. Discuss the turn amount and the shared vertex; do not invent the composition of original source figures.

**Activity 1 (15 min):** *Draw Angles Andre's Way*. The source student-facing wording asks a learner to imagine both clock hands at 12 and turn the minute hand until it reaches 3. Students draw two rays starting at one clock centre. A 12-to-3 clockwise turn covers three of twelve equal clock divisions, a quarter turn. Teacher verifies their sketches, ray directions and partner explanation.

**Activity 2 (20 min):** *Compare Angles on the Clock*. Use the original authorised depicted clock-pair figures (the archived text explicitly mentions 5:00). For each actual source pair, students identify the larger turn and explain. Do not substitute a list of invented publisher clock pairs. In first-party exact-hour examples, 5:00 is 5 shorter hour gaps (150°), 6:00 is 6 gaps (180°), 8:00 is 4 gaps (120°). Note that exactly 5:30 is NOT 5:00: the hour hand moves continuously between marks.

**First-party Cool-down (5 min):** Compare the smaller angles at exactly 5:00 and 8:00. 5:00 is larger by **one hour-mark interval** (30°, if converting to degrees). Explain the turn, not the printed arm length. The original publisher *Which Angle is Larger? By How Much?* is protected and has not been reproduced.
