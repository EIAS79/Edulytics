# Grade 4 — 2021 U06:L15: first-party teacher-led support (not an original IM teaching guide)

The Edulytics learner explanation for **Situations Involving Area** and its area/width numeric examples is already correct. Keep it intact. This document is an independent teacher script for the parts that **cannot** be treated as automatically completed digital practice.

### Warm-up — 10 minutes
Present a *first-party* labelled soccer-field rectangle with width 40 m, area 2,280 m² and unknown length. Students propose three estimates (too low, about right, too high); 1 minute of quiet thought, partner discussion and teacher collection of reasons. The exact missing length is 57 m, but a good estimate is a range or benchmark, not necessarily an exact answer. This is not a license to reproduce the publisher image.

### Activity 1 — 15 minutes
Provide grid paper. Use the 189-tile mural, width 7, and ask for missing length **with equations or a partitioned area model**, not only the numeric answer. Encourage decomposing the area into 140+49, producing partial quotients 20+7=27. Allow independent work and a partner explanation; check the grid preserves seven rows and the correct tile count. Retain teacher observations outside the machine-graded scalar question.

### Activity 2 — 20 minutes
Provide grid paper and sticky notes; ask whether 197 tiles make an exact 6-tile-wide rectangular mural and what greatest whole-number length is possible. The full rectangle uses 192 tiles (6×32) and five remain. A 33rd column needs six tiles, which are unavailable. The teacher compares multiple student representations in a gallery walk, with 3–4 strategy posters. A scalar-only answer is not evidence that the student's drawing/remainder interpretation is correct.

### Synthesis — 10 minutes
Discuss how the product of the sides is area and why partial quotients recover an unknown length, including the role of the remainder in an incomplete mural. Preserve oral and written justifications as classroom evidence.

### First-party Cool-down — 5 minutes
A new independent Edulytics question: a 168 m² rectangular garden is 6 m wide; explain by partial quotients why its missing length is 28 m (168=120+48; 120÷6=20 and 48÷6=8). This is **not** the gated publisher original.
