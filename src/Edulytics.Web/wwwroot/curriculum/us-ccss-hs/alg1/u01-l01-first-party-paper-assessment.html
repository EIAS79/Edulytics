1.1 Four kinds of questions — warm-up

For each prompt, say whether different respondents could reasonably give different answers, and whether the answer would be numerical or categorical. Explain an odd-one-out that you can defend mathematically; more than one answer may be valid.

  1. How many minutes did each classmate spend reading yesterday?
  2. Which sport does each classmate prefer?
  3. How many pages does one specific named book have?
  4. What is the school’s current official name?

1.2 Statistical questions and class survey

Design a numeric statistical class question, a categorical statistical class question, and one nonstatistical fixed-fact question. Write a fourth question of your own. For each statistical question, explain the likely variation and identify how a single respondent could answer.

Privacy: Ask up to 15 classmates ONLY if a teacher authorizes voluntary collection. Do not record names, contact information, health, sensitive characteristics or identifying individual responses. Participation is optional. A hypothetical dataset must be labelled invented and MUST NOT be called collected data. Summarize variation in aggregate.
