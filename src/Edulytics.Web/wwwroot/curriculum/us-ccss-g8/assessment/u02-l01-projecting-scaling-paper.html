First-party dilation model — from projection to scaled image

Use center O=(0,0) and original triangle P=(2,0), Q=(0,2), R=(2,2). Draw rays OP, OQ, OR and enlarged corresponding image points P′=(3,0), Q′=(0,3), R′=(3,3). For every pair, calculate the ratio of distances from O. Identify one common scale factor and show all corresponding sides of the image are that factor times the original. Distinguish this ideal Euclidean dilation from a physical shadow, which may be distorted if projection geometry is not appropriate.

Independent Edulytiks Practice — five source skill families

P1 — Select all scaled rectangles: Original 12×4 rectangle. Which candidate pairs are scaled copies? A=6×2, B=9×2, C=18×6, D=24×8, E=12×6. Calculate both dimension ratios for every choice and justify each accepted/rejected item.

P2 — Compare scaling in both directions: R=10×6, S=15×9 and T=20×10. Find R→S and S→R factors. Explain why T is not a scaled copy of either R or S using both corresponding dimensions.

P3 — Draw three scaled polygons: Draw triangle A with vertices (0,0),(4,0),(0,4) scaled by ½ about origin; rectangle B (0,0),(2,0),(2,1),(0,1) scaled by 2; quadrilateral C (0,0),(8,0),(4,4),(0,4) scaled by ¼. Label EVERY image vertex and explain one matching side-length ratio per polygon.

P4 — Retrieve triangle angle sums: Which proposed ordered angle triples can be interior angles of a triangle? A=(40°,50°,60°), B=(45°,55°,80°), C=(30°,60°,90°), D=(50°,50°,90°). Show total 180° checks and reject each invalid set.

P5 — Retrieve parallel-line angles: Draw parallel lines y=0 and y=3 crossed by transversal y=x, intersecting at (0,0) and (3,3). Label the acute corresponding and alternate interior angles and their adjacent supplementary angles, and give a geometrical reason for the values 45° and 135°.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — identify dilation or distortion

A rectangle A has dimensions 3×2. A same-corner rectangle B measures 6×4 and another C measures 6×3. Draw all three, compare ratios and diagonals from the common corner, and explain which image is the dilation of A, its scale factor and why the other image is NOT similar. Show how a common ratio determines every corresponding side and makes diagonal rays align. This is a distinct first-party Cool-down; it does not reproduce the publisher's teacher task.
