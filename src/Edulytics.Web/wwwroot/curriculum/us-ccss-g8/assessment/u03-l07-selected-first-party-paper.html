Representations of Linear Relationships

7.1 • Which container holds more?

Compare a tall narrow glass, a short wide glass, and a conical glass. First make a labelled estimate, then list what dimensions or volumes are needed to justify a comparison.

Explain why height alone is insufficient to rank container volume. Draw each proposed shape and show a sensible check.

7.2 • Rising water level with a changing starting value

A tank has an initial 5 cm water depth, then rises 2 cm per added identical displacement block. Draw a table at n=0,1,3,7 blocks.

Repeat with initial 11 cm, keeping the same displacement per block. Interpret both slopes and intercepts, and discuss the simplifying model assumptions.

7.3 • Compute slopes from graphs

For line A through (1,3) and (4,9), find slope. For line B through (−2,5) and (2,1), find slope. For line C through (0,−1) and (5,−1), find slope.

Draw slope triangles where possible and show why negative or zero slope is a valid rate.

Independent Practice • Four source-aligned tasks

P1: Draw three linear relationships with different intercepts but one slope 2; write an equation for each.

P2: A bamboo plant starts 18 cm tall and grows 4 cm monthly. Write h(t) and find its height at 7 months.

P3: Recipe A uses 3 bananas for 2 cakes; recipe B uses 7 bananas for 4 cakes. Compare bananas/cake.

P4: Explain a rigid motion and dilation mapping a rectangle 2-by-3 to a rectangle 4-by-6; show corresponding factors.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • From measurements to equation

A first measurement is (2,9) and a second (6,21), where x is blocks added and y is centimeters. Find slope and starting height.

Write y=mx+b and test (10,33); identify the slope and intercept meaning.
