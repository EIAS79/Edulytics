Comparing Proportional Relationships

Edulytiks independent classroom assessment. Teacher scores drawings, discussion and field work; no online or database grading is claimed.

4.1 • Build a situation for y=4.2x

Invent a realistic proportional context with rate 4.2 units of output for one unit of input. Label axes and units.

Construct a four-row table and a graph through (0,0), then explain what 4.2 means and why it is not an intercept.

4.2 • Compare two earnings representations

Worker A earns $8.40/hour and worker B $7/hour. At 12 hours calculate each total and the difference.

Calculate how many completed whole hours each needs to earn at least $150. Discuss why crossing a threshold must round up to the next whole hour.

Show two equivalent representations of the comparison and justify which worker earns faster.

Independent Practice • Rate comparison and slope

P1: Company A hauls 8 tons in 2 hours, company B hauls 15 tons in 3 hours. Compare tons/hour.

P2: Andre walks 6000 steps in 50 min; Priya models steps as y=118x. Who walks faster and by how many steps/minute?

P3: Graph line slope 2/3 through (0,0) and give three distinct point pairs whose connecting line has that slope.

P4: Explain why two rate graphs with differently scaled axes cannot be compared by drawing angle alone.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Compare unit prices

Vendor A sells 9 kilograms at $27; vendor B sells 7 kilograms at $24.50. Determine dollars/kg for each, and which is cheaper.

Find cost for 12 kilograms at each rate and explain what makes the relationships proportional.
