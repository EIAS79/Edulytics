15.1 — Can You Draw It? Nine triangle categories

Fill every combination of SIDE type (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and ANGLE type (acute, right, obtuse). For each possible cell write a valid three-angle set that sums to 180° and sketch a matching triangle; for an impossible cell explain why it cannot exist. Use the inclusive definition of isosceles (at least two sides equal); equilateral triangles also have two equal sides, but should be marked as the more specific equilateral case in the third row. An equilateral-right or equilateral-obtuse triangle cannot exist.

15.2 — Find All Three

Three first-party angle cards describe the SAME triangle under congruent rigid positions. Card A has interior angle at vertex A equal 50°, Card B shows interior angle at B equal 60°, and Card C gives an EXTERIOR angle next to the unknown interior angle at vertex C. Use congruence of the three views and the triangle angle-sum theorem to compute interior angle C, then calculate its supplementary exterior angle. Draw one labelled triangle and explain why a turn, slide or reflection cannot change those angle measures. Do not put the exterior angle inside the 180° interior sum.

15.3 — Tear It Up: make one straight angle

Draw or cut three independently authored corners of a triangle whose interior angles you have determined (or use paper pieces 50°, 60°, and your computed third angle). Put all three vertices at ONE point, arranging the sectors side by side WITHOUT overlaps or gaps so their outer rays form a straight line. Paste/trace the assembled sectors, and justify the 180° sum. Then draw a quadrilateral with one diagonal and explain how two triangle angle sums give the quadrilateral's total 360° interior angle sum.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — a missing interior angle

One Euclidean triangle has two interior angles measuring 50° and 60°. Find its third interior angle and draw a labelled triangle. Reproduce a physical or carefully labelled paper construction of all three angle pieces fitted along a straight line. Explain why the answer follows from the angle-sum theorem, not from approximate measurement of a sketch. This is an Edulytiks-authored alternative, not the inaccessible publisher Cool-down.
