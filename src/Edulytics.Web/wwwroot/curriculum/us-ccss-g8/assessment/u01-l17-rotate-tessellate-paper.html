17.1 — Deducing Angle Measures

At ONE grid vertex, arrange or draw (A) six congruent equilateral triangles, (B) four congruent square tiles, and (C) three congruent regular hexagons. In each case all tile interiors must be disjoint and the full 360° around the common vertex covered with no gaps. Record each regular polygon's interior corner angle and compute number of copies times angle. Then test a regular pentagon with interior angle 108°: do THREE around a vertex leave a gap, and would FOUR overlap? Explain why a single-shape regular pentagon tiling fails this vertex test.

17.2 — Tessellate This

Choose one original tile: a unit square or an equilateral triangle with side 2 units. Construct a repeating first-party planar tessellation of at least 12 copies, drawn as a section of an extendable periodic pattern. No tile interiors may overlap and adjacent matching edges must have equal lengths. Show at least two translations that take the complete INFINITE repeating pattern to itself, specifying each directed vector (for a 1×1 square grid, for example (+1,0) and (0,+1)). Then describe why these motions preserve congruence of all tiles and why the pattern can continue without a boundary gap.

17.3 — Rotate That: non-identity rotational symmetry

Design an original four-tile rotational pattern: place four congruent copies of the same asymmetric triangular tile around O=(0,0), at rotations 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° counterclockwise. Label corresponding vertices and each angle of turn. Verify that a non-identity 90° rotation about O maps the COMPLETE four-copy pattern onto itself, not merely one selected copy. Exchange with a partner: partner identifies center, direction and smallest positive rotation and records any correction to the reasoning. You may use tracing paper and additional labelled grids.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — six equilateral triangles

Draw six congruent equilateral triangles meeting exactly at the same grid vertex O. Mark ALL SIX interior corner angles of 60°, show the 360° total, and draw an arrow for a 60° rotation mapping the six-copy arrangement onto itself. Explain why the shared edges and equal angles prevent gaps and overlaps locally, and how repeated tile translation or rotation extends the arrangement into a tessellation.
