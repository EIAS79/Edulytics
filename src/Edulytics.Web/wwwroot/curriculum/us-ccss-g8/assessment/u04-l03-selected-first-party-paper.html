3.3 • Keep equality and assess peers

A student claims x+5=8 can become x+5−5=8 by changing only the left side; explain why the step fails.

Solve 2x+6=3x−8 by subtracting the same variable term on both sides, then check original equality.

Independent Practice • Five transformations

P1: An unknown x and unknown y satisfy a labelled hanger equality 2x+3y=20. If x=4, find y.

P2: Two students solve 2x+6=3x−8 using different first legal steps. Describe and compare them.

P3: Complete a table for 3x+y=15 for x=0,2,5.

P4: Name the legal operation mapping 5x+2=17 to 5x=15.

P5: Decide which real-world quantities require solutions greater than or equal to zero.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Legal equivalence

A peer solves 5(x−2)+3=3x+1 by subtracting 3x, distributing and dividing. Solve independently and list each legal equivalent move.

Explain why a final numeric answer must still be checked in the original equation.
