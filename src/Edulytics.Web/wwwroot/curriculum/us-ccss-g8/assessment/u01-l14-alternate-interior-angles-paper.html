14.1 — Angle Pairs

Draw two lines intersecting at O. Mark one acute angle as 30°. Label its vertical opposite angle, and both adjacent angles. Calculate their four measures and explain why opposite vertical angles are equal under a 180° turn about O, while adjacent pairs are supplementary because they form a straight 180° angle.

14.2 — Cutting Parallel Lines with a Transversal

Let ℓ and m be distinct parallel horizontal lines. A nonhorizontal slanted transversal crosses them at P and Q. At P, mark one acute angle with the right-pointing horizontal ray as 63°. Sketch the interior region between ℓ and m. Locate and label a corresponding acute 63° angle at Q, one alternate interior acute 63° angle on the opposite side of the transversal, and two adjacent supplementary 117° angles. Explain which equalities depend on the lines being parallel.

14.3 — Alternate Interior Angles Are Congruent: rigid proof and counterexample

Draw original ℓ: y=0 and m: y=3, with transversal t: y=x. The intersection points are P=(0,0) and Q=(3,3). Find their midpoint M. Rotate the entire configuration through 180° about M. Explain why ℓ maps onto m, t maps onto itself, and a 45° interior angle at P maps to its alternate interior angle at Q. Identify each of the corresponding rays in your sketch.

Counterexample: replace m by nonparallel n: y=0.5x+3 while ℓ stays y=0 and t stays y=x. Explain why the transversal now meets n at (6,6) and why the angle between t and n is not automatically 45°; use the unequal slopes or angle direction, not just the appearance of the picture.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — 63° / 117°

A transversal cuts two parallel lines; one acute angle is 63°. Draw the pair of lines and a transversal, then mark ONE correctly positioned alternate interior angle of 63° and ONE adjacent angle of 117°. Give the supplementary calculation and explain why the equal alternate interior result requires parallel lines. Do not treat an arbitrary nonparallel sketch as a theorem.
