8.1 — Building a Quadrilateral from rotated triangles

Draw the original right isosceles triangle O=(0,0), A=(3,0), B=(0,3) on a grid. Its right angle is at O and OA=OB=3 units. Construct three more congruent triangles by turning the entire original through 90°, 180°, and 270° clockwise about the fixed point O (do not successively stretch a copy). Label the four triangles and all rotated A and B positions. Describe the outer boundary: is it a square? Explain its rotated orientation and why there are no gaps or overlaps in the interiors.

8.2 — Rotating a Segment: three different centers

Segment PQ starts at P=(0,0), Q=(2,0). Rotate the ORIGINAL segment through 180° around each center separately: (i) endpoint Q=(2,0), (ii) its midpoint M=(1,0), and (iii) an off-line point C=(0,2). Calculate and label P′ and Q′ each time, and sketch all three new segments. Explain why each image is still length 2, but only the midpoint rotation maps the original segment onto itself as a set.
