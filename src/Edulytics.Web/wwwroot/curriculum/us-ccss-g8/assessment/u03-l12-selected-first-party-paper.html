12.1 • Estimate shaded areas

A first-party 4-by-4 square grid has 12 shaded small cells, another has 12, and a third has 13. Rank their shaded fractions and justify by common square-unit area.

Explain why a drawing's color or shape is insufficient without counting matching square units.

12.2 • Apples and oranges in a two-variable equation

Apples cost $1 each and oranges $2 each. A shopper spends exactly $10. Let a be apples and o oranges, write a+2o=10.

List four feasible ordered pairs of nonnegative whole fruits and one impossible pair. Plot feasible points; explain why negative fruit counts or fractions cannot describe an actual whole-fruit purchase.

12.3 • Solutions and everything else

The sum of twice the first number and the second is 10: 2x+y=10. Determine whether (0,10),(2,6),(5,0),(3,3) lie on the corresponding graph.

Produce at least two additional solutions and one nonsolution, showing exact substitutions and a plotted line. A single equation admits infinitely many real ordered-pair solutions.

Independent Practice • Five source-equivalent mathematical skills

P1: For 2x+3y=6 test (0,2),(3,0),(1,1) and (−3,4); justify correct/incorrect.

P2: A line has equation y=−2x+5; identify its intercept and test (2,1) and (2,2).

P3: For x+2y=8 write three correct integer solutions and one incorrect.

P4: A fuel tank starts with 40 gallons and dispenses 5 gallons per second. Write y=40−5x and realistic time domain.

P5: Four turkey sandwiches cost $33 delivered, eight cost $61. Assuming one flat delivery fee and a constant sandwich price, find the fee and each sandwich price.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Exact ordered pair membership

For 3x+2y=14 decide whether (2,4),(4,0),(0,7) and (4,2) are solutions. Show substitutions; graph if useful.

Explain why an equation in two variables is not answered by finding one isolated number.
