2.2 — How Did You Make That Move? Six-frame transformation sequence

Follow a single asymmetric triangle with ordered vertices P, Q, R. The tip is R. Study the six frames. Between each adjacent frame, name the precise motion (slide vector, turn angle with direction and center, or reflection line). One transition changes the handedness of the triangle even though the tip points in the same direction. Explain why this cannot be just a translation or rotation.

FramePQR (tip)Tip direction

Record five step-by-step moves and any fixed center or mirror line. Compare corresponding side lengths before and after each move.

Explain the unique reflection using vertex order/chirality; do not infer a reflection from the tip direction alone.

Partner: cover the move list, reconstruct frames 1–6 on grid paper from a spoken description, then switch roles. Attach the labelled reconstruction and a note about where directions needed clarification.

2.3 — Card Sort: Move

Cut out or copy these six independently authored cards. Sort into translation, rotation and reflection; there are two of each. Justify each grouping using movement evidence, not merely the names. For a turn identify center and angle, for a flip the mirror line, and for a slide the shared displacement.

Card 1 — Every point moves 2 right and 1 up.
Card 2 — A tracing turns 90° clockwise about marked point P.
Card 3 — A tracing flips across vertical line x=0.
Card 4 — Every point moves 3 left without turning.
Card 5 — A tracing flips across horizontal line y=2.
Card 6 — A tracing makes a half-turn around marked point O.

Group the cards, draw or describe one example from each group, and explain which moves change the orientation of an asymmetric figure.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — Is It a Reflection?

This is a new Edulytiks task; the publisher Cool-down was not available for source verification. Start with triangle P(1,1), Q(3,1), R(2,3). Its proposed image is P′(-1,1), Q′(-3,1), R′(-2,3). Decide whether a single reflection produces that image. If yes, name the mirror line and explain the perpendicular/equal-distance property for all three pairs.

Now consider P″(-1,1), Q″(-3,1), R″(2,3). Could this be the same reflection? Justify. Include labelled sketch or an equally precise verbal geometric construction.

Teacher collection: rotation estimate and explanation, complete six-frame motion list with partner evidence, card sort with reasons, and labelled Cool-down/reflection counterexample.
