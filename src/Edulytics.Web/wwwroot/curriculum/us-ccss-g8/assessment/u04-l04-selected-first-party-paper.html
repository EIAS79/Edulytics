4.1 • Different-looking, equivalent equations

Compare 3(x+4)=27 and 3x+12=27. Explain why the solution set is identical even though the forms differ.

Write two legal steps showing 5x+2=2x+14 is equivalent to 3x=12, and solve both.

4.2 • Critique a written solution chain

Clare solves 2(3x−1)+5=4x+9 and shows 6x−2+5=4x+9, 6x+3=4x+9, 2x=6, x=3. Mark each legal step and independently substitute to check.

A second student jumps from 2(3x−1)+5 to 6x−1+5, then x=2. Explain exactly where distribution failed.

4.3 • Create two independent solution paths

Solve 4(x−3)+2=3x−7 twice: first distribute, second isolate the expression by subtracting an equal term. Compare efficiency.

A partner checks both routes and identifies whether either method silently divided by a possibly zero variable.

Independent Practice • Six source skill types

P1: For (2/5)b+1=−11, determine b by exact arithmetic, checking competing claims −25 and −28.

P2: Solve 3(x−4)=12x and verify the negative fractional value.

P3: Explain legal operations from 5x+6=3x+18 to x=6.

P4: Find the mistake in a claim that 2(x+4)=2x+4, and correct the distribution.

P5: Find a line equation through (5,7) and (8,13).

P6: A ribbon total of 15 meters is cut into x and y meters. Write the relation, draw a graph and state domain.
