10.1 — Angles of an Isosceles Triangle

Original isosceles triangle A=(0,3), B=(-2,0), C=(2,0) has AB=AC. Draw and label it, then reason how a reflection and rotation preserve angle measures: (i) reflect the triangle across line AB and track the image of C; (ii) describe a turn about A that aligns the corresponding ray with AB; (iii) check equality of base angles at B and C by reflecting across the perpendicular bisector of BC, x=0, which exchanges B and C. State which corresponding angles are equal and why. A mere claim that the triangle 'looks balanced' is not a proof.

10.2 — Triangle Plus One: form a parallelogram

Take triangle A=(0,0), B=(3,0), C=(1,2). Let M be the midpoint of AC. Rotate the ORIGINAL triangle 180° about M to its image C,D,A. Compute M and D, then draw quadrilateral A–B–C–D. Show that AB is parallel to DC and BC parallel to AD using displacement vectors. Determine area of triangle ABC and the completed parallelogram, explaining why it is two congruent triangles without gaps.

10.3 — Triangle Plus Two: compare three rigid copies

For scalene triangle P(1,1), Q(4,1), R(2,3), independently construct its translation by (+2,-1) and its reflection across x=0. Label each corresponding P′,Q′,R′ and P″,Q″,R″, then identify at least two equal corresponding side pairs and two equal angle pairs by vertex correspondence. Explain why the reflected copy has reversed orientation without changing any side or angle measure.

10.4 — Triangle ONE Plus: compose two reflections

Construct original triangle ONE with O=(0,0), N=(5,0), ON=OE=5, angle NOE=30°, and E above ON. First reflect across ON (the x-axis); name the image of E as M. Then reflect the resulting triangle ONM across the line OM, naming the image of N as T. Give the lengths OM and OT, and the directed angles from ON to OE, OM and OT. Describe each reflection line and explain why all rays from O retain length 5. An equivalent exact radical-coordinate construction may be provided instead of rounded decimals.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — two rectangle tiles

Join two congruent 3-unit by 2-unit rectangles along one entire 2-unit side with no overlap and no gap. Draw the two tiles and label their shared interior boundary. Find the final outer width and height, exterior perimeter, and area. Explain why the internal 2-unit edge is not counted in the exterior perimeter, even though each original tile has that edge.
