9.1 • Odd line out

Plot A:y=2x+1, B:y=−2x+1, C:y=0, D:y=−x−3. Choose one odd line and justify using slope, intercept, steepness or context.

Provide a different valid odd-one-out argument from a partner; the teacher assesses the reasoning, not only the selected letter.

9.2 • Transportation card decreases

Noah begins with $40 and spends $2.50 per journey. Write remaining balance B(r), create a table for r=0,1,2,10,16, and graph.

State why journey count r must be a nonnegative integer and why the model ends at r=16. Explain why r=17 is not a usable paid ride from the remaining balance.

9.3 • Travel habits across a month

A travel card contains $30 initially, then loses $1.50 after each journey. If two rides occur every school day, plot the balance at day 0,2,5,10.

Explain why this context has discrete steps and cannot be interpreted as arbitrary negative rides or monetary value below zero.

9.4 • A weekly payback plan

Elena owes her brother $96 and returns $12 weekly. Write remaining debt, graph and interpret slope and intercept.

Explain whether a negative or zero balance makes sense after week8.

Independent Practice • Four decreasing models

P1: An aircraft descends from 9000 feet at 600 feet per minute. Write a model and say when the model reaches zero.

P2: A hiker starts at a campsite 4 km from trailhead and walks 1.5 km/h farther along the route. Interpret positive/negative slopes depending on the reference point.

P3: A caller earns $1 per completed call, plus a fixed $5 signing bonus. Is the earnings relation proportional?

P4: Identify slopes of lines through (0,5),(2,1); through (0,−2),(4,−2); and through (3,0),(3,6).

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Understand domain

A balance starts $25 and decreases $5 per ride. Write B(n), find B(3), and state valid whole-number rides until the balance reaches zero.

Explain why a negative balance on the linear extension does not represent another purchased ride.
