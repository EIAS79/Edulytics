16.3 — Every Triangle in the World: parallel-through-apex proof

Draw a triangle A=(0,0), B=(6,0), C=(2,4). Through upper vertex C draw the line ℓ parallel to base AB. Label the angles at A, B and C. By alternating sides of the transversals AC and BC, mark two angles at C along ℓ equal to the base angles A and B. Show why these two transferred angles and the original angle C are adjacent on one straight line and therefore their exact sum is 180°. Explain which part of the proof requires ℓ∥AB and why the argument works for any nondegenerate Euclidean triangle.

16.4 — Four Triangles Revisited

Make a composite from FOUR congruent copies of right triangle with vertices O(0,0), P(3,0), Q(0,3), each rotated successively by 90° about O. Draw and label all four copies so they exactly tile a diamond-shaped square without overlapping interiors or gaps. Explain how the four right angles about O sum to 360°, and then draw a quadrilateral diagonal showing that two triangles establish the 360° quadrilateral interior-angle sum. Distinguish the four 90° rotations from the 180° triangle angle-sum theorem.
