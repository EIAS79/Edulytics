6.1 — Equivalent Expressions

Simplify 10(2+3) − 8×3 and write three different equal expressions using order of operations and distributivity. Explain why they all equal 26. Do not replace the expression with a visually similar but numerically different formula.

6.2 — Similarity Transformations (Part 1)

Triangle: Draw first-party triangle E(0,0), G(2,0), H(0,1) and target triangle L(5,1), M(1,1), N(5,3). Identify and draw a complete ordered sequence consisting of (i) reflection over the y-axis, (ii) factor-2 dilation about origin O, then (iii) translation (+5,+1). Show every corresponding vertex after each move, check lengths and angles, and decide whether these triangles are similar and whether they are congruent.

Hexagon: Original A(0,0), B(2,0), C(3,1), D(2,2), E(0,2), F(-1,1). Construct rotation 90° counterclockwise about O, then dilation k2 about O, then translation (+5,+1). One equal pair of lengths alone is not enough to verify a hexagon's similarity.

6.3 — Similarity Transformations (Part 2): prescribed combinations

Starting with original first-party triangle P(0,0),Q(2,0),R(0,1), make four SEPARATE images of the ORIGINAL: Figure B by translation (+3,+1) then reflection in the y-axis; Figure C by reflection in the x-axis then dilation k2 about O; Figure D by rotation 90° counterclockwise about O then reflection in the y-axis; Figure E by dilation k½ about O then translation (+4,+2). Use the given order of operations, label three corresponding image vertices for every figure, and prove whether each is similar and which are also congruent to original. Explain why dilation factor 2 or ½ changes the side lengths.

6.4 — Methods for Translations and Dilations: two five-card partner sets

Teacher HANDS ONLY Set A to Student A and ONLY Set B to Student B. Each student independently chooses a valid sequence using only their own five cards to map original triangle X(0,0),Y(2,0),Z(0,1) exactly onto target X′(3,1),Y′(7,1),Z′(3,3). Then compare sequences orally, identify which center/factor/translation differs, and justify why both solutions are valid. Record actual chosen cards and the intermediate labelled vertices; teacher collects an observation of the discussion. The two card sets are separately printable first-party alternatives, not copies of publisher cards.

Independent Edulytiks Practice — three source skill families

P1 — Similarity via a sequence: Construct triangle P(0,0),Q(2,0),R(0,1) and image P′(4,2),Q′(4,6),R′(2,2). Find one correct combination of a rotation, dilation and translation mapping each corresponding vertex; show all three transformed coordinates and scale factor.

P2 — Verify all side and angle correspondences: The source quadrilateral ABCD has A(1,9),B(1,6),C(3,5),D(2,6) and a proposed similar image E(9,9),F(9,3),G(5,1),H(7,3). Find exact transformation x′=11−2x,y′=2y−9, show a rigid reflection, common dilation 2 and translation that realize it, and demonstrate two corresponding side lengths and all polygon angle invariants.

P3 — Dilation from a larger nested triangle to a smaller one: Large triangle A(0,0),B(6,0),C(0,6) contains triangle D(0,0),E(2,0),F(0,2). Show center O and factor ⅓ sending ABC to DEF. Explain why factor 3 would be the inverse and why the two triangles are similar but not congruent.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — demonstrate similarity with full images

Triangle A(1,1),B(3,1),C(1,2) has proposed target D(5,2),E(9,2),F(5,4). Describe one complete sequence from ABC to DEF: dilate k2 about origin then translate (+3,0). Draw and label every image point; verify each corresponding side and angle, and state why an equivalent reordered sequence would require changing a translation vector or the center.
