Writing Equations for Lines

11.1 • Coordinate differences and lengths

On an independent grid plot A(1,2),B(5,2),C(5,8). Find horizontal AB and vertical BC; calculate slope AC using rise/run.

Explain the effect of reversing both direction differences and why the slope is unchanged.

11.2 • What an equation says about points

Use the line through (0,0) and (4,3). Decide by exact substitution if (20,15), (100,75) and (90,68) lie on it.

Write a general equation valid for all points on this line and explain how a single incorrect point fails the criterion.

11.3 • Relationships from slope triangles

Use coordinates (2,1) and (6,7), construct a labelled slope triangle and find slope and intercept.

Write an equation for the entire line, verify both original points and derive another point on it.

Independent practice: line equations and similarity

P1: Find slope through (−1,3) and (3,11).

P2: Find the equation through (0,4) and (2,7), and verify (4,10).

P3: Two students get slope 6/4 and 1.5; determine if they agree and explain mathematically.

P4: A right-angled rectangle is compared with a four-sided figure having one 105° corner. Prove why the two polygons cannot be similar.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Verify the equation

A line has slope 2 and passes through (1,−1). Find its equation and verify (4,5).

Reject (4,6) by substitution and explain why a point 'looking near' the line does not qualify.
