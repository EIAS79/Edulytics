11.1 • Which pair of lines differs?

Sketch pairs: (a) y=−4 and y=−2, (b) x=3 and x=−1, (c) y=−4x and y=−4, and (d) y=x+1 and y=2x+1. Give two valid classifications based on parallelism, slope and intercept.

Explain why valid different odd-one-out arguments may exist; teacher assesses the reasoning rather than only the letter.

11.2 • Ten points with constant coordinate

Plot ten distinct points whose y-coordinate equals −4; find the equation and its slope.

Plot ten distinct points whose x-coordinate equals 3; write the equation, classify its slope and explain why this is NOT y=3 or y=3x.

Discuss why neither line has a one-to-one output for every x when the line is vertical.

11.3 • Same perimeter rectangles

Draw ten pairs of nonnegative lengths and widths for rectangles with perimeter 50 units. Make a table and graph the relation between length l and width w.

Derive l+w=25 and w=25−l. Explain positive domain 0<l<25 for nondegenerate rectangles and why the line has slope −1.

Independent Practice • Line types and modeling

P1: Graph y=−4x−1, find y-intercept and two points.

P2: Draw horizontal line through (2,5) and vertical line through (2,5); write equations and slopes.

P3: Explain why y=3 and x=3 are different sets of points.

P4: A book has two covers each 1/4 inch thick and N pages each of thickness 0.002 inches. Write T(N) and predict N=250.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Lines through one point

Give equations of the horizontal and vertical lines passing through (−2,7). Draw and label each.

Explain which has slope zero and which has undefined slope, and verify two additional points on each line.
