7.1 Odd Equation Out

Compare x=x, 2x=6, x+1=x+2 and 5(x+2)=5x+10. Give two different supported odd-one-out classifications.

Explain which cases are identities and contradictions.

7.2 Thinking About Solutions

Classify n=n, n+3=n+1, 2n=0 and 7(n−2)=7n−14.

Prove each by algebraic simplification instead of checking just one input.

7.3 What's the Equation?

Choose a making 3x+6=3(x+a) true for all x; choose another a making it impossible.

Create an equation with unique solution x=−2, show its derivation.

Seven practice question families

P1: Classify 5(x+1)=5x+5 versus 5(x+1)=5x+6.

P2: A student claims 2x+2=x+1 has no solution. Check x=−1.

P3: Simplify (6x−10)/2−x.

P4: Explain why multiplying an identity by 4 and adding 31 preserves all solutions.

P5: Compare legal first steps for x/2+3=7x/2+5.

P6: Solve 4(x−1)=3x+9.

P7: A line of slope4 passes through (−3,6). Find intercept.
