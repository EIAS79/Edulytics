8.1 ? Equivalent numerical expressions

Use each of the numbers 4, ?2, and 10 exactly once to construct three structurally distinct expressions with value 20. Do not replace ?2 by +2. Show evaluation using order of operations. After working alone, compare representations with a partner.

Expression A: __________ Expression B: __________ Expression C: __________

Explain why the expressions are equivalent even though their parentheses or multiplication order differ.

8.2 ? Construct and compare triangles with one fixed angle

Teacher supplies a private angle strip with one angle marked A (for example 40?). On paper build a triangle containing that angle using rulers or pasta strips. Measure all side lengths and angles; allow measurement tolerance of ?5?.

Find two classmates with the same assigned A angle. Record their triangle measurements and decide whether every pair is similar, congruent, neither, or uncertain. Explain why one shared angle does NOT establish similarity.

Attach labelled triangle drawings or physical construction evidence. The teacher records partner interaction and evaluates the explanation.

8.2 ? Compare triangles determined by corresponding angles

Teacher supplies angle-strip A = 40?, B = 60?, C = 80? (or another valid total of 180?). Construct one larger and one smaller triangle whose corresponding angles match. Measure, label, and compare them.

Record pairs of corresponding angles, three corresponding side ratios, and one possible translation/rotation/reflection followed by dilation that aligns the two triangles.

Prove why two corresponding congruent angles force the third pair to match; discuss why tape/ruler measurement noise does not invalidate exact AA similarity.

Extension: In triangle PQR, choose S on PQ, then draw ST parallel to QR meeting PR at T. Explain using corresponding angles why PST is similar to PQR for any interior S.

8.3 ? First-party pentagon triangle investigation (optional source role)

Use the original Edulytiks construction below. The outer polygon is a regular pentagon and O marks its center. Draw two smaller triangles by connecting O to vertices and by drawing one diagonal. Identify two triangles whose corresponding angles can be proved equal (using symmetry or a parallel auxiliary line), and state the order of matched vertices.

Draw an additional triangle that is not similar to your chosen reference; justify with an angle counterexample or inconsistent side ratios. Do not rely on appearance alone.

Teacher judges the full labels, geometry and discussion. The source's named diagram is not copied; this is a first-party replacement of the AA/pentagon reasoning modality.

Independent Grade 8 practice (five skill families)

P1: Triangles A and B have angle sets (53?, 71?, __) and (53?, 71?, __). Find the missing angles and justify AA similarity.

P2: Triangles C and D each have a right angle; their other given angles are 37? and 53? respectively. Decide whether they are similar by matching the FULL angle multisets, not the order they are listed in.

P3: Two different-sized equilateral triangles have side lengths 3 and 7. Are they congruent? Similar? State the factor mapping the small to the large and explain.

P4: In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC with D on AB and E on AC. Given AB=12, AD=8, AC=15, calculate AE and justify similarity by two angles.

P5: Parallelogram PQRS has side PQ=5 units. After dilation by factor 3, what is the length of corresponding side P?Q?? Explain why angle measures do not triple.

P6: A quadrilateral Q has vertices (1,1),(3,1),(3,2),(1,2); P has vertices (4,2),(8,2),(8,4),(4,4). Describe a dilation followed by translation mapping Q to P and verify each vertex.

P7 (extension): Two quadrilaterals have equal angle measures 240?,40?,40?,40?. Must they be similar? Why does triangle AA not apply to arbitrary quadrilaterals.

Explain which conclusions are numerically checkable and which drawings or justifications require a teacher.

Triangle P shares only the 42? angle with M and has another angle of 75?. Is P guaranteed similar to M? Explain with the third-angle sum and a counterexample.
