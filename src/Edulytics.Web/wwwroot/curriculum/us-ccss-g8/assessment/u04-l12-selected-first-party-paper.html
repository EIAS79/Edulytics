12.1 • Milkshakes

One milkshake starts at 12 oz and decreases by 1/4 oz per second. Another starts at 20 oz and decreases by 2/3 oz per second. Write two linear equations.

Find exactly when the volumes are the same, check both values, and note the meaningful domain before either drink becomes empty.

12.2 • Passing on the Trail

Two hikers travel along an 18-km straight trail from opposite ends at 3 km/h and 2 km/h. Write their distances from the parking lot versus t.

Graph and calculate their meeting point/time; distinguish a single intersection from parallel movement.

12.3 • Two Stacks of Cups

One stack height is h=1.5n+6 and another h=1.5n+9, with n≥1 whole cups. Can they have the same height for the same n?

Prove why equal slopes and distinct intercepts yield NO simultaneous solution; provide example of coincident lines having infinitely many solutions.

Four system application families

P1: Draw line y=2x+1; select another line through (0,−3) that intersects it once.

P2: Create a second equation so y=3x+4 has no solution together with it.

P3: Broccoli costs $2/lb and zucchini $1.50/lb; purchase 10 lb for $17. Find both weights.

P4: Celsius and Fahrenheit satisfy F=9C/5+32. When does numerical Fahrenheit equal Celsius? Verify.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Prove no intersection

Graph y=2x+1 and y=2x−3 on the same axes and explain why no point satisfies both.

Replace the second equation with 2y=4x+2 and classify the new system.
