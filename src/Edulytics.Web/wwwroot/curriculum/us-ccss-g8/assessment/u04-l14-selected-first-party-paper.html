14.1 ? Mental substitution

Solve mentally: x=5 with y=x-7; y=4 with y=x+3; and x=8 with y=-11.

State what value was substituted and why the remaining equation became a one-variable equation.

14.2 ? Choose efficient systems to solve

Without solving first, sort several systems by expected difficulty based on whether a variable is isolated and whether equations share a convenient form.

Solve four representative systems: y=4, x=-5y+6; y=-3x+10, y=-2x+6; 3x+4y=10, x=2y; and x+y=10, x=2y+1. Check every ordered pair.

14.3 ? Detect contradiction before solving

Explain why x+y=5 and x+y=7 cannot both be true for the same x and y.

Create a different pair of linear equations that reduces to a false statement such as 0=4, and explain why that proves there is no solution.

Six aligned practice families

P1: Solve y=6x with 4x+y=7.

P2: Solve y=3x with x=-2y+70.

P3: With y=-1.5x+3, choose a second line giving exactly one solution and justify using slopes.

P4: Modify one coefficient or constant in x-6y=4 and 3x-18y=4 to create one-solution and infinite-solution variants.

P5: Match equations y=?2x?3 to graphs using slopes and intercepts.

P6: Find the slope through (-3,4) and (1,7).

Original Edulytiks Cool-down ? Substitute and justify

Solve y=2x+5 and x+y=11 using substitution.

Check the solution in both equations, then describe one change to the second equation that would make the system have no solution.
