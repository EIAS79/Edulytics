Understanding Proportional Relationships

Edulytiks independent classroom assessment. Teacher scores drawings, discussion and field work; no online or database grading is claimed.

1.1 • Compare two motion graphs

Graph motion g through (6 cm,3 s) and motion f through (8 cm,2 s), both through origin, with distance on x-axis and time on y-axis. Label units.

Calculate seconds/cm and inverse cm/s for each. Which motion is twice as fast? Explain why a visually steeper graph here means slower motion.

1.2 • Moving through representations

A robot moves 2 cm/s and a beetle moves 4 cm/s. Create a 0–4 second distance table, corresponding tick diagrams and correctly labelled time-versus-distance graphs.

Write an equation for each motion and interpret points (1,2) and (1,4).

1.3 • Twice as fast

Compare positions after 5 seconds for objects travelling at 3 and 6 cm/s. How much time does each need to travel 18 cm?

Explain which quantities double and which are halved when speed doubles. State the units.

Independent practice • Four source-aligned skills

P1: A runner covers 5 km in 25 min; graph the runner and another travelling twice as fast.

P2: A farm charges $16.50 for 6 lb berries. Calculate dollars/lb and price for 10 lb.

P3: Find the line slope through (−4,1) and (4,6), and explain why its graph does not model a proportional relationship.

P4: Graph y=(3/2)x and verify two integer coordinate pairs.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Interpret a unit rate

A walker covers 36 meters in 12 seconds. Give meters/sec and sec/meter. Draw graph with time on x-axis and distance on y-axis.

What is the meaning of (1,3)? Explain why y=3 cannot model nonzero walking speed.
