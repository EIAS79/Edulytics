On or Off the Line?

Edulytiks independent classroom assessment. Teacher scores drawings, discussion and field work; no online or database grading is claimed.

10.1 • Odd lines in the plane

Graph y=x+1, y=−x+7, y=2x and y=4. Provide two different mathematically justified odd-one-out comparisons.

Distinguish having a common y-intercept from being parallel or intersecting.

10.2 • Pocket Full of Change

A purse contains 17 coins, all quarters or dimes, with total value $2.00. Define q and d. Write q+d=17 and 25q+10d=200 (cents).

Solve for both integer counts and verify both constraints. Explain why a fractional coin would not be feasible.

10.3 • Making Signs

Andre has made 15 signs and Clare 5 signs by yesterday. Today Andre makes 3 per hour and Clare 5 per hour. Model each cumulative amount and find when the totals match.

Graph both lines. Discuss the meaning of intersection and why their starting amounts differ.

Five practice question families

P1: Lines m:y=x−10 and n:y=−x+2. Determine whether (6,−4) lies on one, both or neither.

P2: Complete 4x−4=4x+__ so the equation is always true, and then impossible.

P3: Mai earns $7/hour mowing and $14 once for recycling. Write earnings and evaluate at six hours.

P4: Clear denominators in x/3+1/2=7/6 without changing solution set.

P5: A hall seats 50 people using two-seat and four-seat tables. Write 2a+4b=50 and find two feasible sets of table counts.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • One, both, neither

Check if (2,5), (8/3,19/3), and (3,8) satisfy y=2x+1 and x+y=9. Classify each as on one, both, or neither.

Identify the intersection and provide a labelled graph and substitutions.
