Representing Proportional Relationships

3.1 • Mental multiplication

Evaluate 15×2, 15×0.5, 15×0.25, and 15×2.25 without a calculator. Show how distributive reasoning confirms a result.

Describe a useful multiplication strategy and correct a common decimal-placement mistake.

3.2 • Represent a walking ratio

Jada takes 8 steps while Noah takes 10, and takes12 while Noah takes15. Let x=Jada's steps and y=Noah's steps. Build a table containing the origin and these pairs.

Find the proportional equation, graph it, and interpret unit rate y/x using correct people/units.

3.3 • Teacher-distributed information gap

Teacher privately hands the problem card to one learner and the data card to another. Neither may show the paper. The problem-holder must ask for the missing quantitative facts.

Explain which facts are necessary to construct a proportional graph. Teacher observes actual questions and verbal reasoning; a fake online chat does not replace the activity.

Independent Practice • Four related forms

P1: A food contains 90 calories in 30 grams. Write calories as a function of grams and sketch a graph.

P2: Ten fundraiser tickets bring in $24. Write money versus tickets, including a 25-ticket prediction.

P3: Explain from a graph how to tell if slope is above, equal to or below 1 when axes use equal units.

P4: Consider y/(x−2)=3/11. Produce two points and explain why this line is not proportional through origin.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • One context, three models

Five packs contain 35 identical cards. Write a table, equation and line graph for number of cards c versus packs p.

Determine c at 12 packs and reject the model c=7p+5 by checking its origin and initial conditions.
