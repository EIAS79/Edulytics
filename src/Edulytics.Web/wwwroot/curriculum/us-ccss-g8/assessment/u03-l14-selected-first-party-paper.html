14.1 • Buying fruit with a fixed budget

Grapes cost $2.39/lb, bananas cost $0.59/lb, and a shopper budgets exactly $15. Let g,b be measured pounds and write 2.39g+0.59b=15.

Find two feasible nonnegative pairs (g,b) using exact decimal arithmetic, and show why g=10,b=1 is impossible under that budget.

A savings account starts at $50 and adds $20 per full week. Write y=50+20w with whole-week domain, and calculate weeks 0,4,9.

14.2 • Five original savings lines

Draw five savings-account relationships on a labelled grid: a=50+20t, b=30+10t, c=110−5t, d=40, and e=10+30t, with t weeks.

Compare which start highest, whose amount decreases, which is constant and which reaches zero at t=22. Explain why graph extrapolation may be invalid for negative balances.

14.3 • Fish-market daily spending constraint

The market buys tilapia at $3/lb and salmon at $5/lb with a $210 budget. Let t and s denote pounds; write 3t+5s=210.

Find two feasible nonnegative whole-pound orders and one infeasible order, checking amounts and explaining what the graph's intercepts mean.

Explain why the line includes possible fractional pound purchases if sold by weight, while negative weights are impossible.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Feasibility of a model

An event sells adult tickets at $8 and child tickets at $5. A group spends exactly $90. Write a two-variable equation and give two feasible integer purchase combinations.

Reject the pair (10 adults,4 children) by substitution, and explain why negative-ticket solutions are mathematically possible but contextually invalid.
