13.1 • Notice and wonder about shadows

Observe the original Edulytiks drawing of two upright objects under parallel sun rays. Write two observations about shadow length, height or direction, plus one question.

Explain which measurements must be collected before predicting a height; a picture alone cannot establish real-world length.

13.2 • Object and shadow ratios

At one instant, a 1.5 m survey stick casts a 2 m shadow; a 0.9 m sign casts a 1.2 m shadow. Calculate both height-to-shadow ratios.

A lamppost casts an 8 m shadow at that instant. Estimate its height under level-ground and parallel-sunlight assumptions, and explain your estimate.

Consider whether a measurement taken an hour later can be mixed with these data.

13.3 • Why corresponding triangles are similar

Draw an upright object, its shadow along level ground and the sun ray joining the top to the shadow tip. Draw a second object-shadow triangle.

Identify the two right angles and a second equal angle from parallel sunlight rays; justify AA similarity rather than only stating ratios.

Explain how nonlevel ground or different measurement times could invalidate the comparison.

13.4 • Teacher-observed field measurement

Working with the teacher outdoors, measure one accessible object height and its shadow while simultaneously measuring a taller object's shadow. Label units, dates and any measurement uncertainty.

Calculate an estimate of the taller object's height and compare with another group. Teacher records whether the observation was real; do not claim supplied model data are physical measurements.

If no supervised measurement is possible, clearly label your work as a simulated calculation and leave the field-observation box unverified.

Independent Edulytiks practice — additional first-party tasks

P1: A 2 m post casts a 2.5 m shadow. Estimate height of an object with a 10 m shadow at the same time.

P2: A 1.6 m person casts a 2 m shadow. Estimate height of a building whose shadow is 12 m.

P3: State an AA argument using vertical poles and nearly parallel sun rays; give a counterexample where the shadow model is unreliable.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • One proportional estimate

A 1.2 m rod casts a 1.6 m shadow; a flagpole casts a 6.4 m shadow at the same moment. Find flagpole height and show a proportion.

Explain why this comparison must use a consistent ground level, units and observation time.
