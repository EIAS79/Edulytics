12.1 — Translated Images

Original triangle P(1,1),Q(3,1),R(2,3) has four proposed ordered images. Select all and only the pure translations of the original triangle. For each selected candidate give one common vector shared by P→P′, Q→Q′, R→R′. For every rejected candidate name a rigid movement that explains its difference, and why it is not a pure translation (same displacement for all vertices).

12.2 — Congruent Pairs (Part 1)

Original quadrilateral A(1,1),B(4,1),C(4,3),D(1,3). Candidate U has A′(-4,3),B′(-1,3),C′(-1,5),D′(-4,5). Candidate V has A″(-4,3),B″(-1,3),C″(-1,6),D″(-4,5). Determine which image is congruent to the original and construct a single movement for it. For the other candidate, show exactly which side or angle cannot match. Check all FOUR corresponding vertices, not just the bottom edge.

12.3 — Congruent Pairs (Part 2)

Asymmetric convex quadrilateral E(0,0),F(3,0),G(3,2),H(1,3) is proposed with two ordered images. Image M reflects all four vertices across x=0: E′(0,0),F′(-3,0),G′(-3,2),H′(-1,3). Image N instead lists E″(0,0),F″(-3,0),G″(-3,2),H″(-1,1). Decide whether M and N are congruent images of EFGH. For M, explain how the reflected vertex ordering changes; for N identify the exact changed side(s) or angle(s) and why no rigid motion can produce the mismatch.

12.4 — Building Quadrilaterals from four equal pieces

Use four first-party unit-square tiles (each 1×1), with no gaps or overlaps, to build a 2×2 square and a 1×4 rectangle. Draw and label the four original pieces in each construction. Both composites have area 4 but their external side lengths differ. Can a sequence of translations, rotations and reflections map the FIRST finished quadrilateral exactly onto the SECOND? Explain using all corresponding boundary sides and angles. Then redraw a translated or rotated copy of your 2×2 square and give a valid sequence that DOES map the square onto that congruent copy.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — translate a rectangle

Translate rectangle A(1,1),B(4,1),C(4,3),D(1,3) by vector (-5,+2). Record all four image vertices and draw the result. Explain why the four side lengths 3,2,3,2 and all four 90° angles are unchanged, and why the vertex order is preserved.
