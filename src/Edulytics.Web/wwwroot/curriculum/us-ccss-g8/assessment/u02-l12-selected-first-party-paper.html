12.1 • Find the missing center

A dilation of factor 2 sends A(2,1) to B(5,4). Find the center C of dilation, showing why A,B,C are collinear and C must satisfy B=C+2(A−C).

Check your center by applying the dilation formula to both coordinates.

12.2 • A line equation from two points

A line passes through (5,3) and (7,7). Find its slope, then write both a point-slope and a slope-intercept equation.

Check whether points (9,11), (100,193), and (9,10) are on the same line using substitutions. Discuss why equivalent equations describe one line.

12.3 • Dilated slope triangles

Let A(0,1), B(2,1), C(2,2). Draw this slope triangle and dilate about A by factors 5/2 and 9/2, writing image coordinates.

Prove that the new C positions lie on the same line y=1+x/2. Explain why slope does not depend on scale.

Independent practice: membership and transformations

P1: Determine which points lie on the line through (0,5) and (2,8): (4,11),(5,10),(6,14),(30,50),(40,60). Show substitutions.

P2: Find the line through (1,−2) and (3,2).

P3: Dilate the triangle (0,0),(2,0),(0,3) by factor 3 about the origin. Label image vertices.

P4: Explain which parallel relationships a positive dilation preserves and why independent scaling of x and y is not a dilation.

For y=2x−7, test points (9,11) and (9,10), and show exact subtraction with units.
