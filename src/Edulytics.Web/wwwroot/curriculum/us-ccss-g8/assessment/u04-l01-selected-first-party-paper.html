Number Puzzles

Edulytiks independent classroom assessment. Teacher scores drawings, discussion and field work; no online or database grading is claimed.

1.1 • Notice and wonder about the number line

Place −8, −3, 0, 2 and 7 on an original number line. Describe two patterns involving equal distances and negative directions.

Explain why a number-line sketch cannot by itself prove a claimed algebraic equality.

1.2 • Telling temperatures as number puzzles

A temperature is multiplied by 2 and then 7 degrees are added, giving 25 degrees. Find the initial temperature. Write and solve an equation.

A starting temperature loses 4 degrees, then the result is tripled and equals −6 degrees. Calculate the initial temperature and check every step.

Compare 2(x+7) and 2x+7; explain how verbal punctuation changes the equation.

1.3 • Exchange a three-step puzzle

Write a first-party puzzle with at least three sequential algebraic operations and a unique solution. Privately write its answer on a separate page.

Give only the puzzle to a partner, collect their written solution and compare its reasoning with your own. Teacher checks ambiguity and checks every inverse operation.

Independent Practice • Seven skill families

P1: A student reads 2/15, 1/3, 2/9 of a book on three days, then 3/4 of the remainder. If 28 pages remain, find the total book length by a fractional equation.

P2: A number is tripled, then 8 is removed to obtain 19. Find the original.

P3: Mai scores 6 points, Noah triples Mai's points, Tyler has 4 fewer than Noah, Elena doubles Tyler. Calculate each.

P4: Test which points (0,−3),(2,5),(3,9) satisfy 4x−y=3.

P5: A nested cart row measures 4 feet for one cart and adds 1.5 feet per extra cart. Write length for n total carts.

P6: Isosceles triangle has two equal angles x and third y. Give an equation connecting x and y, and solve for y when x=55°.

P7: Graph y=2x+1 and y=−x+7 and determine their intersection.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Read the words precisely

A student thinks of x, adds 4, multiplies the sum by 3, and subtracts 2 to obtain 25. Write the equation and solve.

An incorrect student uses 3x+4−2=25. Explain the missed parentheses and check both numerical predictions.
