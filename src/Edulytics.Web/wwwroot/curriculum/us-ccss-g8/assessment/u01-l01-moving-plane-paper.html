1.1 — Which angle does not belong?

Four first-party angle figures are described: A forms a straight 180° angle; B forms a right 90° angle; C forms an acute 45° angle; D forms an obtuse 125° angle. Pick one and explain what makes it mathematically different. More than one choice can be justified. A letter without a property is not enough.

1.2 — Describe and reconstruct a six-frame dance

Use the provided Edulytiks first-party A/B/C triangle motion figures for this lesson (not publisher cards). Draw the same asymmetric triangle on squared paper, mark point P and its tip. Secretly pick one sequence. Give a partner clear slides by distance and direction and quarter-turns by direction; the partner reconstructs all six frames and identifies your card. Swap roles. Paste or attach both six-frame drawings and written move lists.

Record the selected card only AFTER your partner has attempted the reconstruction. Explain how each step maintains side lengths and angles.

Independent Edulytiks Cool-down (not the protected publisher task)

An asymmetric triangle has reference point P=(1,1), tip pointing north. Slide the entire triangle right 3 grid units without turning; then turn the triangle 90° clockwise around the new P. Draw the start, translation and final pose with the tip direction. State final P, tip direction and explain which lengths and angle measures are unchanged.

Attach labelled three-stage drawing; if giving a verbal alternative, make it precise enough to reconstruct.
