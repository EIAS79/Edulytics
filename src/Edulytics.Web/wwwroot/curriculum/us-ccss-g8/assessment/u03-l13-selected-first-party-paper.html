13.1 • Coordinate pairs from selected values

For each equation 6x=7y; 5x+3y=9; y+5−x/3=7, choose two x-values then calculate matching y-values and record exact ordered pairs.

Verify (7,6), (0,3), and (6,4) respectively. Explain why a point that solves one equation may not solve another.

13.2 • Are graph points solutions?

Draw lines y=2x+1, y=−x+7, and y=3. Check points (1,3),(4,3),(0,3) for each relation with exact substitution.

Explain why a visual point near the line is not enough, and why a point at the intersection of two lines solves both equations.

13.3 • Teacher-distributed equation/data cards

Teacher cuts the six equation cards and six data cards independently before distribution. The student holding an equation asks their partner for missing x or y information and proves a match.

No learner may inspect the entire master during the activity. The teacher observes actual partner exchange, including correction of mismatched cards.

Independent Practice • Five source-aligned skills

P1: For y=2x+5, calculate y when x=−3 and x when y=2.

P2: Check if points (6,13),(21,33),(99,137) all satisfy y=(4/3)x+5. Explain why this is not guaranteed by a straight-looking graph.

P3: Given y=x/4+5/4, determine two integer-coordinate points and one nonsolution.

P4: A rectangle with A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(5,10),D=(0,10) has AB=5 and BC=10; verify every vertex in coordinate format.

P5: Compare a proportional line through origin with a nonproportional affine line when matching graphs to real situations.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • One point, several equations

Check whether (3,2) satisfies 2x+y=8, 5x+3y=9 and 3x−2y=6. Record true/false with exact arithmetic.

Explain why a point on one line may fail an unrelated line and describe how to find a point common to two equations.
