7.2 — Are They Similar? Two false claims

Example A: A square with all four sides 2 and all angles 90° is compared with a rhombus with all four sides 2 but angles alternating 60° and120°. Priya says equal side lengths prove similarity. Clare says angle mismatch disproves similarity. Draw both, choose the valid reasoning, and explain why dilation and rigid motions preserve angle measures.

Example B: First-party rectangles R=4×2 and S=6×4. Jada says they are similar because each side length increased by 2. Lin suggests independent side factors1.5 and2, then translation. Is either claim mathematically valid? Show BOTH ratios 6/4 and4/2, compare all four right angles and explain why ONE common scale factor is necessary. Give a valid contrasting scaled rectangle T=6×3, from original R by factor1.5.

7.3 — Find Someone Similar: original partner card search

Teacher distributes ONE of these separate cards to each student; students circulate to find someone whose polygon is SIMILAR BUT NOT CONGRUENT. Do not inspect other cards before discussion. Partners record matching corresponding sides, angle types, scale factor and which rigid movement (if any) aligns the figures. Cards A–F form three pairs; card G is a distractor. Verify each valid pairing with positive factor other than 1, and explain why G is not a match for cards A–F. Teacher observes whether the partner search and explanation actually occurred.

Independent Edulytiks Practice — six source skill families

P1: Triangle ABC is dilated by factor2; its largest original angle is82°. Find the largest image angle and explain why no angle doubles.

P2: Draw a 3-4-5 right triangle and a 6-8-10 right triangle in different positions/orientations. Establish similarity using corresponding ratios and angles; justify why they are not congruent.

P3: Draw two rectangles 2×3 and4×5 that both have four right angles but are NOT similar. Demonstrate the two unequal corresponding side ratios, and explain why visual resemblance is insufficient.

P4: A triangle has side lengths 3,4,5; a similar image has corresponding longest side10. Find the other two image sides, labelled a and b, show the common factor and explain why an unscaled picture is not a measurement.

P5: Source skill: invalidate a proposed dilation using mismatched angles. Original right angle B=(1,0),C=(0,0),D=(0,1) has vertex C=90°. A proposed image B′=(3,0),C′=(0,0),D′=(1,3) has a different angle at C′. Decide whether ANY single-center positive dilation could produce it, and give two mathematical reasons using angle or noncollinear ray correspondence.

P6: Draw horizontal segment A(0,0),B(2,0). Rotate original AB 90° CCW about A and independently 90° CW about B to locate the two upper square vertices (0,2) and(2,2). Give a final rigid transformation using a translated copy of AB to complete the square and justify side lengths and right angles.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down — look beyond angle similarity

A 3×5 rectangle is compared with a 6×10 rectangle and a 6×9 rectangle. All have four 90° angles. Use side correspondence in the same orientation to determine which is a scaled copy of 3×5, identify the single factor and prove why the other rectangle is not similar despite having matching right angles. Draw labelled rectangles and write both ratios; this is first-party material, not the publisher's protected 7.4 Cool-down.
