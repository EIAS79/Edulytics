Keeping the Equation Balanced

2.1 • Hanging socks — notice and wonder

Sketch a hanger holding two 3-g weights on its left and one 6-g weight on the right. Describe what the picture establishes and what cannot be inferred.

Explain why a physical hanger must have equal arms and equal total masses to stay balanced.

2.2 • Hanging blocks

Original first-party hanger has two identical unknown bags and a 3-g square on the left, and an 11-g block on the right. Represent it as 2x+3=11.

Remove 3 g from both sides, then split the remaining mass between two equal bags. Label each stage and check the final mass x.

2.3 • More balanced hangers

A triangle weighs 3 grams and a circle 6 grams. On a hanger, three triangles and one unknown square balance two circles. Solve for the square.

Explain why removing only from the left breaks balance, while removing the same weight from both sides preserves equality.

Independent Practice • Five source skills

P1: Which hanger changes preserve equality: adding 2g on both sides, subtracting on only one side, doubling only one side, dividing both sides by 2?

P2: Three identical bags and a 4-g weight balance 19 grams. Solve.

P3: Two squares and a triangle of 4 grams balance 18 grams. Find square mass.

P4: Andre is three years younger than brother and two years older than sister. Write expressions for age relationships and discuss information missing to find a number.

P5: Draw two parallel lines y=2x+3 and y=2x−1; compare slope and intercept.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down • Keep both sides equal

Solve 4x+5=21 and produce a labelled hanger diagram. Explain which two legal operations give x.

A peer subtracts 5 from only one side and reports x=21/4. Explain the error.
