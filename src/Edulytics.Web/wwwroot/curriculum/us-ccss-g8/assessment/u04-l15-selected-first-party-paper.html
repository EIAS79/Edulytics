15.1 ? Classify systems before solving

Classify each as no solution, one solution, or infinitely many: two distinct lines with slope -4/3; lines y=4x-5 and y=-2x+7; equations 2x+3y=8 and 4x+6y=17; and y=5x-15 with y=5(x-3).

Explain which structural feature?slope, intercept, or equivalent equations?determines each classification.

15.2 ? Turn situations into systems

Write a system for two travelers with different start times and rates; state what the solution means without solving first.

Write a system comparing two rental plans with a base fee and hourly rate.

Write a system for a fixed total number of two ingredients in a given ratio.

Write a system for two bulk goods with a total weight and total cost. Define every variable and interpret the ordered-pair solution.

15.3 ? Information-gap reasoning

Partner A receives a question but incomplete numerical data; Partner B receives the missing data. Ask only for information needed to define two equations.

After exchanging information, both partners solve independently, compare equations, and explain why the shared solution answers the original context.

15.4 ? Mixed system-solving practice

Choose four systems from a mixed set containing isolated variables, standard-form equations, parallel lines, and contextual rate equations.

Before calculating, identify which method?equal expressions, direct substitution, or structural classification?should be most efficient for each.

Five cumulative practice families

P1: A worker earns r dollars per hour and a senior worker earns 30% more; use their hours and combined pay to solve for r.

P2: Decide which story matches y=x+6 and x+y=100 and explain each equation.

P3: Write and solve a two-variable scoring system from two players' totals.

P4: Solve y=6x-8 and y=-3x+10.

P5: From an intersecting graph, select two matching equations and solve their system exactly.

Original Edulytiks Cool-down ? Write, solve, interpret

A school event sold 10 tickets: adult tickets cost $2 and student tickets cost $3, for a total of $24. Write a system, solve it, and interpret both coordinates.

Explain how you know the same ordered pair satisfies both constraints.
