The original edge point E is halfway along AB; point F is one-quarter of the distance along AD starting at A. 13.2 — Congruent Ovals

Sketch an original oval with FULL major-axis length 4 and FULL minor-axis length 2, centered at O=(0,0), major direction horizontal. Candidate V is the same oval turned 90° about O, major direction vertical (lengths 4 and 2). Candidate W instead has full horizontal major length 5 and vertical minor length 2. Which candidate is congruent to the original and why? Identify the rotation taking original to V and explain why no translation/rotation/reflection can resize the major axis to length 5. Do not confuse full axis lengths with semiaxis radii.

13.3 — Corresponding Points in Congruent Figures

Original triangle A(0,0),B(4,0),C(0,3) is first translated (+2,+1), then reflected across x=0. Find all image vertices. Find the image of midpoint M of AB and the interior centroid G=(A+B+C)/3, not just the polygon vertices. Explain why both M and G must follow the SAME rigid transformation and how this strengthens a congruence argument.

13.4 — Astonished Faces

Draw a first-party circular face of radius 2 centered O=(0,0), eyes L(-1,1), R(1,1), nose N(0,0), mouth M(0,-1), plus a distinguishing cheek marker S(1.5,0.5). Face X is the exact reflection across x=0 of every feature, including S. Face Y has the same mirrored eyes, nose and mouth but its cheek marker is at (-2.5,0.5) instead of the correctly reflected position. Which face is congruent under the stated labelled correspondence? Justify with the rigid reflection and the distance from nose to cheek marker. A visible expression alone is not sufficient.
