Anchoring Units of Measurement

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

2.1 • Benchmark size

Compare a 15-centimeter pencil, a 1-meter desk, a 200-milliliter cup and a modeled 2.5-centimeter-edge container. State whether you are describing length, mass or capacity/volume and whether the unit size is realistic.

2.2 • String length

A two-yard string measures6 feet or72 inches. Lay out an estimated yard or describe it using marks, then measure it with a ruler. Record the signed difference between estimate and measurement and discuss why one physical reference is useful.

2.3 • Card sorting

Sort measurement labels for a pencil, lake, refrigerator, hamster and swimming pool. Explain your choices; several units may be valid if their scale is handled correctly.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

A rope is2 yards long. Express it in feet and inches. Explain whether the rope's length should be measured in centimeters or cubic centimeters and why.

Teacher rubric: 2yards=6feet=72inches. Centimeters measure length, cubic centimeters measure volume. Teacher reviews measured benchmark, selected unit reasoning and the student's explanation.
