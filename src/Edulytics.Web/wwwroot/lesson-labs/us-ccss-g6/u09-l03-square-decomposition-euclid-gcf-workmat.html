Square Decomposition and Equivalent Fractions

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

3.1–3.2 • Cut largest squares first

Draw a21-by6 rectangle. Remove three6-by6 squares, leaving3-by6; then remove two3-by3 squares. The last square side3 reveals GCF(21,6)=3.

3.3 • Equivalent fractions through shapes

Compare9×4 and27×12. The larger rectangle is three times each edge; both ratios width/height equal9/4=27/12=2¼, though last squares have sides1 and3 respectively.
