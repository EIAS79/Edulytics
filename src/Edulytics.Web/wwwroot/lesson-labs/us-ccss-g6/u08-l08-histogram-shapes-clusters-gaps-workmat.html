Describing the Shape of Histograms

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

8.1–8.2 • Spot distribution features

A histogram may have a single peak, several clusters, empty intervals, symmetry, or longer tails on one side. Describe what you can actually see rather than invent missing values from a sample size.

Compare two histograms

Five equal binsFrequenciesCharacter
A1,4,6,4,1Symmetric, central peak
B4,0,0,3,0Gap between clusters

Sketch both with adjacent equal-width bars and label where any zero-height bars occur.

8.3 • Context before prediction

Discuss why song lengths might cluster while daily phone talk times could have many small values and a long right tail. State why these are predictions until actual data are collected.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Two histogram patterns for five equal bins are A=1,3,5,3,1 and B=5,1,0,0,2. Describe each shape, any gaps, and which has a central peak.

Teacher rubric: A symmetric central peak, B has zero-height gap in middle and uneven clusters. Teacher assesses clear graphical explanation. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
