12.2 — The original filing cabinet

The original lesson's cabinet is 24 × 12 × 6 sticky-note units. Cover the top, two front/back faces and two side faces; do not cover the bottom resting on the floor. These original source measurements differ from the separately labeled independent 8 × 5 × 3 example in Edulytics.

12.3 — Unit cubes, visible and hidden faces

Build a 2 × 2 × 3 prism from twelve unit cubes (surface area 32), then build a different prism with twelve cubes. Draw all six faces including the three that cannot be seen in one viewpoint.

For this 3 × 2 × 5 prism, the complete area is 2 × (3×2 + 3×5 + 2×5) = 62 square units. In one picture you only see three faces. Count their opposites too.

Original practice 3 displays five-cube L and plus shapes. In both drawings four face-sharing pairs hide eight of the original thirty cube faces: 22 outer unit squares each. Neither shape has a greater surface area.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down (not publisher 12.4)

Using the original teacher-preparation dimensions, build or sketch a 2-by-3-by-5 unit-cube prism. Label the surface area of all six faces (including back and bottom), give the total 62 square units, and explain why multiplying 2 × 3 × 5 gives the number of cubes, not the surface area.

Teacher rubric: 3D prism truly uses 30 cubes; opposite-face pairs are 2×3,2×5,3×5; correct sum is 62 square units; hidden faces counted; distinguishes volume (30 cubic units) from surface area (62 square units); written justification and model independently teacher reviewed. The numerical check cannot certify the model.
