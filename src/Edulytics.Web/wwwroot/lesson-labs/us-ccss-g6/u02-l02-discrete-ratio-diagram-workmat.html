Represent ratios with labeled diagrams

Make your own groups. Every marker stands for one unit of the quantity named in its row. Unlike measurements stay labeled (cups, tablespoons, teaspoons): never silently treat unlike units as identical physical units.

Activity 2.1 • Quarter and dividing by four

24 ÷ 4 = 6 and one quarter of 24 = 6. Explain why those expressions give the same count.

Activity 2.2–2.3 • Two-row diagrams

Write two complete sentences describing the ratio, retaining each unit. Trade your diagram with a partner and check their category order.

Activity 2.4 • Three-ingredient card sort

Example: 2 cups raisins, 4 cups pretzels and 6 cups almonds. Use twelve equal markers, grouped and labeled by ingredient. Ordered raisins:pretzels:almonds = 2:4:6. Give a different two-category ratio using the same diagram.

Another first-party model: 4 cups sauce, 2 tablespoons oil, 1 teaspoon spice. Use distinct symbols and the correct units in a three-row display. Compare with a partner and discuss whether a swapped label can make a statement false.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

There are three four-legged animals and two two-legged animals. Show a labeled discrete diagram of their legs, then state the ratio of four-legged legs to two-legged legs. Explain your grouping.

Teacher rubric: 12 legs from the first group and 4 from the second, so 12:4; labels must name legs, not animal counts. Review drawn groups, units, ordered ratio sentences and reasoning. The numerical Practice response does not automatically certify an authentic student sketch.
