Grade 6 · Unit 1 · Lesson 19 — Design a Tent

An Edulytics-designed geometry project. There is no single correct tent shape or fabric amount. Numeric feedback below checks only two worked examples. The teacher evaluates every student's actual design, space for campers, panel dimensions, fabric estimate and written explanation.

Part 1 — Plan a floor-inclusive tent for up to four campers

Draw a 3D tent and a net, choosing shape, number of campers, height and dimensions. Include a floor; account for roof and end walls. Sketch four sleeping bags with realistic dimensions inside your proposed floor and state any assumptions about free headroom, aisle space, doorway and seams.

Worked example: four 2-by-6-foot sleeping bags side by side, with no aisle, require floor 8-by-6 feet. A tent of width8ft, height3ft and length6ft has sloping length5ft.

Source-dimensional check — actual sleeping bags

The original reference diagram specifies a 74-inch-long by 34-inch-wide sleeping bag (about 6 ft 2 in × 2 ft 10 in). Four side-by-side bags need 4×34 = 136 inches of width before an aisle. The 2-by-6-foot model above is retained as a separate, idealized arithmetic example; it does not fit these actual dimensions.

One independently designed floor that fits these bags is 12 ft wide × 7 ft long (144×84 inches). That leaves only 8 inches total width and 10 inches length beyond the bags, so it still provides no comfortable aisle. Choose a triangular cross-section width12ft, perpendicular height4.5ft and half-base6ft, giving a 7.5ft sloping roof side.

Part 2 — Compare and justify your designs

Compare your design with another drawing for the same number of campers. Discuss which uses less fabric, whether both include the floor, why roof slope and tent height matter, how much sleeping space is provided and which assumptions are uncertain. A claim with no measured panels and no calculation is not sufficient.

Student response: write dimensions, surface decompositions, units and a defense of the design. The blank space is for a paper sketch or attached teacher-reviewed drawing.

Edulytics independent Cool-down

Using your own design, write the area of the floor, roofs and end panels. Add them in square units, explain how four campers fit, and defend one trade-off against a peer's design. There is no universal numeric answer.
