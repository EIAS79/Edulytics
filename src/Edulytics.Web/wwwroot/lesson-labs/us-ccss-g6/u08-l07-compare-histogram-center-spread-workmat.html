Histograms, Center, and Spread

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

7.1–7.2 • Compare centers and spreads

Two class histograms show messages sent in a week. Compare typical bins and how far the measurements extend. The sixth-grade plot covers a broader range than the seventh-grade plot, but source image counts need direct verification.

7.3 • Construct a narrower histogram

Create a histogram for a second group of40 runners whose typical time is around10 minutes and whose values are less variable than the given group. For example use frequencies5,15,15,5 in four consecutive one-minute intervals from8 to12. Explain why its distribution clusters near10.

Bounded number of dimes

Four through nine dimes give40,50,60,70,80,90 cents. None of three dimes or ten dimes fits the strict constraint over30cents and under100cents. Draw an open-endpoint interval.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Compare datasets A=5,7,9,11,13 and B=8,9,9,9,10. They share a typical value near9. Which has greater spread, and how would their histograms differ?
