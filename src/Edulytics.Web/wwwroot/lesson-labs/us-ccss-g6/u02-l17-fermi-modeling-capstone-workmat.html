A Fermi Problem

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

17.1 • Fix It!

Suppose a student estimates the number of pupils as24 classrooms times25 students, but calls600 an exact school census. Explain what must be fixed: distinguish a mathematical model from a measurement, and identify sources of uncertainty.

17.2 • Thinking like a modeler

A Fermi problem is deliberately difficult to measure directly. Break it into smaller questions and choose plausible assumptions, units, and bounds. Example: estimate the total pencils used by a school in a term from the number of classrooms, students per class and pencils per student.

17.3 • Your own research problem

Propose at least five open-ended estimation questions and choose one for teacher approval. Record why the question is useful, the data you would need, an explicit lower/middle/upper assumption, arithmetic, and an annotated diagram or table. Present your reasoning, including why a different set of assumptions might produce a different defensible answer.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Independent formative reflection (not an original publisher Cool-down): Given a modeled school with24 classrooms and20–30 students per classroom, find the lowest and highest estimated total student count and describe why neither is a confirmed census.

Teacher rubric: Bounds480 and720 students. Teacher checks all units and assumptions, explicitly labels the values as estimates, and reviews the student's independent Fermi question and presentation.
