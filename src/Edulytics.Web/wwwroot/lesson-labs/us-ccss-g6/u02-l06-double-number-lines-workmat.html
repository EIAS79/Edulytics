Introducing Double Number Line Diagrams

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

6.1 • Adjusting a factor

Why do 2:3,4:6 and6:9 describe the same color proportions? Explain using the number of repeated batches.

6.2 • Drink mix

One batch uses 2 cups yellow and 3 cups red. The two parallel rows below show aligned tick positions after each complete batch.

Draw two parallel continuous number lines and keep equal tick distances on each. Align values in each column.

6.3 • Paint and cookies

A purple recipe uses 3 cups red and 2 cups blue. Mark 6:4 for two batches,12:8 for four batches. Then create two new aligned axes for cookies: flour 0,5,10,15,20 cups and eggs 0,3,6,9,12.

Explain what an aligned tick means, and why your numerical scales differ despite matching horizontal positions.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

One cookie batch requires5 cups flour and3 eggs. Draw a labeled double number line from0 to4 batches. Mark all flour and egg counts and state the pair at 4 batches.

Teacher rubric: Both rows have ticks at the same batch positions: flour0,5,10,15,20 and eggs0,3,6,9,12, with correct units. Teacher checks equal spacing, alignment and student-generated lines. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
