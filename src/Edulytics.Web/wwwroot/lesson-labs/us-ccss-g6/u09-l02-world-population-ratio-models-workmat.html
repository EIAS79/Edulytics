If Our Class Were the World

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

2.1 • Historical ratio model

Imagine a30-student class representing the source-era estimate of7.4billion people worldwide. Fourteen represent those whose main food is rice,12 are under20, and5 represent Africa. Calculate remaining counts, percentages and approximate world correspondences.

2.2 • Questions that require assumptions

Draft three questions starting 'How many people in the world…?'. Decide which are measurable with historical counts and which need a survey or additional estimates. Clearly mark that7.4billion is a historical classroom approximation, not a live count.

2.3 • Represent a small class

Scale world fractions to a25-person class. With a16% share,4 students represent that category. For an approximate10% share,2 or3 students are reasonable whole-number choices. Draw a class diagram and justify rounding.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

If4 of25 classmates represent a group, what percent do they represent? If an approximate world group is10%, explain why a25-person class may represent it with2 or3 students.

Teacher rubric: 4/25=16%. 10% of25=2.5; use whole counts2(8%) or3(12%), both approximate. Teacher reviews rounding explanation. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
