Recipes: scale every ingredient

Independent Edulytics activity using first-party diagrams. Keep the units and ingredient order visible. Draw or describe each repeated batch; a teacher checks the reasoning, not only the final numbers.

3.1 • Flower pattern

Create a flower using three triangular petals and two circular details, then make two complete copies and a third. Draw grouped petals rather than random extra parts. What features stay in the same ratio?

3.2 • Drink mixes

One recipe uses one cup water and four teaspoons powdered mix; two batches need two cups water and eight teaspoons mix. The recipe tastes the same under the same mixing and ingredient assumptions. Explain why doubling just the water changes the concentration.

Practice: one cup of juice concentrate for every three cups sparkling water. Draw two other combinations with the same taste, and one that should taste different.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Bird-food recipe: one batch takes 4 tablespoons seeds and 2 tablespoons oats. Make 3 batches. Write the two scaled quantities and explain why the ratio stays the same.

Teacher rubric: 12 tablespoons seeds and 6 tablespoons oats; compare 4:2 to 12:6 using three equal groups. Check source task diagrams, same-taste reasoning, complete unit labels, two different equivalent ratios, and one non-equivalent ratio. The automatic practice checks selected numbers only.
