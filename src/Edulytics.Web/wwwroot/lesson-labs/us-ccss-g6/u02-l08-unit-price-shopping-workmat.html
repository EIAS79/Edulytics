How Much for One?

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

8.1 • Per one

A price per one item is the total divided by the count of equally priced items. State each unit explicitly: dollars per ticket, cents per apple or dollars per pound.

8.2–8.3 • Shop with a double number line

Draw aligned number lines for ticket count and cost. Explain how the unit price 12 dollars per ticket predicts the remaining counts.

Market unit costs

12 eggs for $3, 3lb peanuts for $7.50, 4 rolls for $2 and 10 apples for $3.50. Calculate each price per one and state the dollar/cents conversion. Compare two different sizes at a common unit.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Five kilograms of rice cost20 dollars at a constant unit price. Find dollars per kilogram, the price for3kg, and explain in a labeled table why 3kg cannot cost20 dollars at the same rate.

Teacher rubric: The price per kilogram is4 dollars;3kg costs12 dollars. Teacher checks price/quantity labels, the division step, a correctly proportioned drawing or table, and that price remains proportional. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
