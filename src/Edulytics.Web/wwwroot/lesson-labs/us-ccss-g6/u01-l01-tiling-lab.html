Tiling the Plane — explore area and coverage

These are original Edulytiks diagrams, not publisher figures. This optional learning workmat is not the graded Practice activity. It does not submit, save or grade drawings or explanations.

1. Compare the four patterns

All four targets are 2 × 2 = 4 square units. In a pattern, a blue square is one piece covering 1 square unit; each yellow right triangle is one piece covering ½ square unit. Tile count is not covered area.

Which one does not belong? Give at least two defensible criteria (for example coverage and the number of tile types). Multiple explanations may be valid.

2. Build a tiling on a 2 × 2 target

Each cell can contain a whole square, two half-square triangles, one triangle, a gap, or an overlapping square and triangle. Try the examples, then use the four selectors to build your own arrangement.

3. Eight hexagons: count pieces, then compare area

The historical lesson asks for area comparisons of colored polygon tiles. This first-party numerical model uses one small triangle as one triangular area unit, not one square unit.

Eight groups with 3 trapezoids, 4 rhombi and 7 small triangles in each
Color and shapePieces in 8 groupsArea of each pieceTotal triangular area units
Red trapezoids8 × 3 = 243 triangle units72
Blue rhombi8 × 4 = 322 triangle units64
Green triangles8 × 7 = 561 triangle unit56

Most pieces: green (56). Greatest area: red (72 triangular units). The total colored area is 192 triangular units, and rotating or translating unchanged pieces does not change their individual areas.

4. Teacher review — not automatic scoring
