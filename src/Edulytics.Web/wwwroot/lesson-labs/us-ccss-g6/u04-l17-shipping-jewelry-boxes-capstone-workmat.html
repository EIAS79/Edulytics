Determining Shipping Costs

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

17.1 • Define the shipping problem

There are270 necklace boxes, each1¾ by2¼ by¾ inches. Compare four classroom shipping cartons with the provided dimension sheets. Historical example prices: small $6.80, medium1 $13.45, medium2 $13.45, large $18.75. The amounts are source-period mathematical inputs, not live postal prices.

17.3 • Compare, revise and explain

Check whether combining one medium1 and one medium2 box can hold276 boxes at a total of26.90 dollars. Discuss unused slots, changing orientation, possible mixed packing, packaging materials, and the difference between a feasible good plan and a mathematically proven global optimum. Submit diagrams and explanations for teacher review.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Independent reflection, not an original numbered Cool-down: If one fixed-orientation medium carton holds144 jewelry boxes, how many identical cartons are needed for270, how many slots remain empty, and why would packing material affect the real result?
