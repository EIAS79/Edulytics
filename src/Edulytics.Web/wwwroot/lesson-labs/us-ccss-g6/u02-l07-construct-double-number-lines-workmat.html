Creating Double Number Line Diagrams

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

7.1–7.2 • Build two axes

Use two parallel, equally spaced number lines for a repeated batch. For every 1 teaspoon cinnamon there are2 tablespoons sugar. Label the axes, the shared zero and the units.

Number of batches012345
Cinnamon (tsp)012345
Sugar (tbsp)0246810

Draw these as number lines, not just a table. Explain why the tick spacing is constant on each axis.

7.3 • Mix and match units

One meatloaf needs2 lb beef and½ cup bread crumbs. At four batches the values align at8 lb and2 cups. A fruit punch recipe uses4 cups pineapple juice,5 cups orange juice, and⅓ cup lime per batch: graph or describe batches 1,2,3 on three labeled axes.

7.4 • Choose a representation

A painter uses2 cups red and7 cups white for one batch. Mark the row aligned at14 cups red and49 cups white. Compare your discrete and double number line diagrams, and choose one to explain to a partner.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

A cat has2 ears and4 paws. Create two double number lines for ears and paws from0 through3 cats. Explain what each aligned tick means and find the pair for3 cats.

Teacher rubric: Ear line0,2,4,6 and paw line0,4,8,12 for cats0,1,2,3. Equal spacing, correct units and a complete per-cat explanation are required. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
