Representing Fraction Division with Tape Diagrams

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

6.1 • Whole-group versus partial-group counts

Use a tape from0 to3 and mark each quarter: there are12 groups. Then regroup into equal3/5 segments to see5 groups.

6.2 • Draw and describe a tape

To model1÷5/6, divide one whole into sixths and group five sixths at a time. There is one complete group and one sixth remaining, which is one fifth of a full group: 1⅕ groups total.

6.3 • Equations as inverse multiplication

The expressions1÷4/5=? and ?×4/5=1 describe the same problem. The result is5/4 groups. Explain why4/5÷1 is a different question, and show each in a correctly scaled diagram.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

How many groups of two thirds fit into two wholes? Draw a labeled tape diagram and show the multiplication check.

Teacher rubric: 2÷(2/3)=3 groups, and3×2/3=2. Teacher reviews three equal-size 2/3 segments and the reference whole. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
