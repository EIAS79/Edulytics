10.2 — A different perpendicular height for each base

Print or copy the figure. For each choice a, b and c, mark that side as the base and draw its matching 90° height from the opposite vertex to the base line. Extend the base line when necessary. Use an index card's right-angle corner as the construction tool.

Teacher check: each chosen side is a real triangle side; the height is perpendicular to the same chosen side, reaches the opposite-vertex line, and is not simply a slanted edge.

An obtuse triangle — the height may be outside

Use the same perpendicular-distance rule when the altitude meets an extension of the base rather than the segment itself. Explain why a long diagonal side is not automatically the height.

Edulytics independent Cool-down

Construct a non-right or obtuse triangle of area 12 square units. Choose a base of 6 units and find the required perpendicular height. Draw that height, show a right-angle mark even when it falls outside the triangle, and explain why different slants can have identical areas. This task is not the original publisher 10.4 "Stretched Sideways" text.

Teacher rubric: 1. Triangle is non-right if the student claims so. 2. Base of 6 and matching perpendicular height of 4 are labeled, with correct location or extension. 3. Area is 6 × 4 ÷ 2 = 12 square units. 4. Student explains the corresponding base-height relationship and equal area across different slants. Numerical feedback alone cannot certify points 1, 2 or 4.
