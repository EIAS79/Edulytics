Statistical Questions and Variability

First-party Edulytics learning activity. Numerical examples are precise; student-created diagrams and explanations receive teacher review.

2.1–2.2 • Questions and responses

A question is statistical when responses from the population are expected to vary. Classifying one fixed count does not become statistical solely because its answer is a number.

2.4 • Define a sample and units

To estimate typical driver red-light waits, collect many durations in seconds; to estimate common athlete heights, gather heights with length units. For football fans, collect yes/no responses and count categories. Explain expected variation.

Independent Edulytics Cool-down

Students ask how many minutes they usually wait for their school bus. Is this a statistical question? Identify the variable and its units, and describe what data should be collected.

Teacher rubric: Yes, different days/students may have varying wait times; numerical variable wait duration in minutes. Teacher checks explanation of variability and sample. Automatic Practice checks selected numbers only. Teacher verifies learners' diagrams, scale, annotations, units and verbal justifications separately.
