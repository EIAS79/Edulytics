Ratio language: collections and color

Independent Edulytics learning activity. Describe what each quantity means. The order of a ratio matters. Drawings, categories and explanations require teacher review.

1. Sort a collection

Sort 10–50 objects into two or three named groups. You may use objects or an accessible written description.

CategoryObject count

Write two sentences: “For every ___ [first group], there are ___ [second group].” Reverse the order and explain what changed. Include a part-to-whole comparison.

Worked example: 9 bananas, 4 apples, 3 plums. Bananas:apples=9:4; plums:apples=3:4; apples:plums=4:3; bananas:plums=9:3=3:1.

2. Shade 24 equal square units

Select a color and click squares until you have 16 color-A and 8 color-B units. Your 24-unit model then has A:B=2:1.

A: 0; B: 0; uncolored: 24

Now draw a different, non-24-unit shape. For example, 18 equal square units can be split 12 and 6. Explain why 16:8 and 12:6 both describe 2:1 even though the shapes have different areas.

3. Independently authored Cool-down

A box contains 8 green counters and 6 red counters. Write green:red, red:green, and green:total ratios. Give one complete “for every” sentence and describe the groups with a labeled diagram.

Teacher rubric: green:red=8:6=4:3; reverse=6:8=3:4; green:total=8:14=4:7. Check labeled grouping, correct order, equivalent ratio explanation, correct 24-grid shading, differently sized second figure and oral/written reasoning. These are not certified by the automatic numeric check.
